How the monk was taken apart

The classic voice is a source–filter sound in disguise. Each grain is three decaying sine waves, one per formant, restarted every pitch period. That is the impulse response of three resonators struck once per glottal pulse. A pulse train through three resonators is a voice with an idealised throat.

So the physical engine asks what body has those resonances. A vocal tract is a tube. Its shape (tongue, jaw, lips) sets where it rings. Its length scales every ring together, and the pharynx and mouth scale separately. We fit one body and five mouth shapes so the tube rings at the classic monk's fifteen formants:

Everyone else is that same gesture performed by another body, not a pitch-shifted copy. A soprano has a shorter tube, with most of the difference in the pharynx. Her folds close softly (head register) and she opens toward "ah" as she climbs, so her first resonance follows her pitch. A countertenor keeps a man's tube but sings with the folds barely touching (falsetto). Every singer has its own glottal jitter, vibrato, pitch wander and breath.

  • Gyuto chant. A fundamental near 65–70 Hz. Each monk tunes F1 onto the 5th harmonic and F2 onto the 10th by listening to his own resonances, so one voice sounds a chord.
  • Half tone (dzo-ke, kargyraa). The ventricular ("false") folds vibrate once for every two glottal pulses and partly block every second one. Flow alternates, and the sub-octave appears.
  • Khöömei. Pressed phonation keeps the glottis closed longer, so resonances ring sharper. The tongue slides one resonance onto a chosen harmonic.
  • Sygyt. The tongue tip seals behind the teeth. The cavity in front clusters F2 and F3 into one narrow peak, the whistle.

Numbers checked by the test suite in this page's source: the closed-form tube response matches the time-domain waveguide to 10−13; scaling a body by 0.8 raises every resonance by exactly 1.25; Gyuto locking settles at 327/654 Hz for a 65.4 Hz fundamental; khöömei holds harmonics 8–12 of 110 Hz at 8–10 dB above their neighbours, and sygyt holds 10–12 at 17 dB.
